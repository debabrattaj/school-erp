What's included

Everything the LMS covers

Courses in three shapes

A course is self-paced, blended, or a plain reference shelf. Blended adds instructor-led sessions with attendance taken against them; the reference shelf has no sequence and no completion at all. Tie a course to a class and section, or leave it school-wide for something like staff induction.

Sections and ordered lessons

Build a course from sections of ordered lessons. A lesson can be written text, a link, a video, a document — or a pointer to study material, a SCORM package, an online test, an assignment or a session, so nothing has to be duplicated to appear in a course.

Prerequisites and completion rules

Prerequisites work at course and lesson level. Each lesson states how it is deemed finished rather than having it inferred: opening it, handing something in, reaching a minimum score, or the learner ticking it off. Optional lessons stay visible and completable but never block what comes next.

Progress that means one thing

A single percentage per enrollment, recomputed from lesson progress on every change rather than incremented. One figure, one meaning — not two counters that drift apart.

A study material library

Documents, video and external links, and written notes published to a class or one section, optionally tied to the syllabus unit they belong to. Material stays invisible to families until it is published and its available-from date arrives, so a teacher can prepare a whole term in one sitting.

Who has opened it, and who has not

Every resource records which students have read it. That is the question a teacher actually asks before a lesson, and it is a single lookup rather than a show of hands.

Homework handed in

Students — or a guardian, recorded as such — hand in text and an attachment, which on a phone means a photo of the page. One submission per student, replaced rather than piled up on resubmission. Lateness is stamped at submit time, so moving a due date later cannot un-late work that was handed in late.

Grading, with the whole class in one view

Submitted, late, and not handed in at all — on one screen, with marks and feedback entered inline. A submission freezes once graded, so a mark can never end up attached to work that was swapped out afterwards. The grade and the feedback reach the family in the portal.

Instructor-led sessions

Blended courses carry scheduled sessions with their own attendance, so a course that mixes independent work with real teaching is one record rather than a spreadsheet beside a platform.

Course feedback

Collected from the learners who took the course, against the course itself — useful for a training programme you intend to run again next year.

On the phone, for both sides

The Android and iOS app carries a Learning tab and hand-in inside Homework, with the same permissions as the web. Teachers publish and grade from the classroom; families read material and hand work in without a desktop.

The same database as everything else

A course result sits on the same student as attendance, fee status and the report card. No second student list to maintain, no export to reconcile, no student who left in August still enrolled on a course in December.

Off by default — switched on per school

Two things we will not turn on for you

Both of these are built and ready. Neither arrives enabled, because each one commits the school to something it should choose on purpose.

SCORM packages

Upload a SCORM package and it runs inside the portal, with completion and score reported back onto the lesson like any other. Off until a school switches it on — accepting uploaded web content and serving it back is a real storage and hosting commitment, not something to inherit by default.

Course discussion forums

A discussion thread per course for the students taking it. Off by default on purpose: student-visible discussion is a safeguarding decision for the school to make deliberately, not a switch that arrives already flipped.
