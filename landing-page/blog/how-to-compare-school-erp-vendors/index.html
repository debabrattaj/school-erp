How to Compare School ERP Vendors: A Side-by-Side Evaluation Framework

A structured way to run a fair side-by-side comparison between school ERP vendors, instead of relying on marketing pages alone.

Published August 14, 2026

Marketing pages aren't built for comparison

Every vendor's site (including ours) is written to make their product look good — which is fair, but it means you can't fairly compare two vendors by reading their homepages side by side. You need the same test applied to both.

Run the same test against every vendor

  • Ask both for a live demo using a class structure similar to yours — not sample data.
  • Ask both the exact same list of questions (see our 10 questions to ask before buying).
  • Compare total cost including migration and support, not just the headline subscription price.
  • Email both support addresses with a real question before committing, and time the response.
  • Ask both what happens to your data if you switch away later.

Weight the criteria by what actually matters to your school

A large school with dedicated IT staff might weight self-hosting flexibility highly; a small school might weight included support and migration much higher. There's no universal winner — there's a best fit for your specific situation.

Build a scorecard before you take the first call

The reason vendor comparison goes wrong is not dishonesty — it is that the criteria get written after the demos, which means they get written to match whatever impressed you most recently. Write the scorecard first, weight it for your school, and score every vendor against it including the incumbent option of changing nothing.

A workable weighting for a single-campus K-12 school: daily-use modules 30%, total cost including migration and support 20%, data isolation and security 15%, parent self-service 15%, support responsiveness 10%, exit terms 10%. Adjust it, but decide the weights before you see a product.

Demo the boring modules, not the impressive ones

Vendors demo dashboards because dashboards demo well. Your staff will spend their year in three screens: marking attendance, entering marks, and recording a fee payment. Insist on watching those three, done end to end, by you rather than by the salesperson.

Bring five real tasks and ask each vendor to complete them live: mark attendance for a full section; enter marks for one subject and generate one report card; record a part payment and produce the receipt; add a new student mid-term and place them in a class; and pull last year's attendance for a student who has left. The tasks that take three clicks with one vendor and a support ticket with another are where the real difference lives.

Cost questions that surface the actual number

Headline per-student pricing is the least useful figure in this category. Six questions produce the real one:

  • Is migration of our existing records included, and what exactly does it cover?
  • Is support included for as long as we are a customer, or for an initial period?
  • Which modules are add-ons licensed separately? In Schoolment, online tests, exam proctoring and biometric attendance are the licensed add-ons; the rest of the suite is switched on per school.
  • Are there per-transaction fees on fee collection? Direct-to-UPI settlement to the school's own account avoids a gateway cut entirely.
  • What happens to the price when enrolment grows by 30%?
  • Are updates included, or is a version upgrade a project?

Test the things you cannot see in a demo

Four checks that predict the next three years better than any feature list:

  • Support latency. Email the support address with a real, specific question before you sign, and time the response. Do it on a weekday afternoon and again on a Saturday.
  • Reference calls. Ask for two schools of similar size using the modules you care about, and ask them what broke and how long it took to fix.
  • Migration reality. Send a sample of your actual messy data — the spreadsheet with merged cells and inconsistent names — and ask what they would do with it.
  • Exit terms in writing. Can you export all of your own data, in what format, and does that survive the end of the contract?
