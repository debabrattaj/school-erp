Back-to-School Checklist: Setting Up Your ERP for the New Academic Year

A practical checklist for setting up your school ERP correctly at the start of a new academic year, before the term gets busy.

Using this guide

The checklists and worked examples in this guide are recommended school procedures and evaluation questions. They are not a promise that every step is automatic. Confirm the modules enabled for your school and rehearse the workflow before relying on it.

Schoolment provides the modules described in the product sections. Reviews & Approvals adds controlled result releases, payment exception review, register submission and saved evidence or timetable versions. Hosting, offsite backups, restoration tests, support response times and complete migration exports require separate deployment or service confirmation.

Before the new academic year starts

  • Confirm the new academic year is set up correctly, with promotion from the previous year applied for every continuing student.
  • Review and update fee structures for the new year — new class fees, revised amounts, new categories if any.
  • Re-confirm staff and role assignments, especially for any new hires — grant only the access each role actually needs.
  • Check that any scheduled automation (fee generation, promotions, exam creation) reflects what you actually want running this year, not defaults left over from last year.
  • Communicate the parent portal to any new admissions — this is often the first real interaction new parents have with your systems.

Do this before term gets busy, not during

Setup mistakes made in week one tend to compound — an incorrect fee structure or a promotion that missed a section is much easier to fix in a quiet week before classes start than three weeks into term.

Where to check each of these

Academic Years for promotion, Fees & Payments for fee structures, and Users & Access for staff roles — all covered in more detail in our Help Centre.

Two weeks out: the academic year setup

Everything else depends on this being right, and it is the item most often rushed. Before anything is promoted:

  • Create the new academic year and confirm its start and end dates match your actual calendar.
  • Check class and section structure for the new year — new sections added, discontinued ones retired.
  • Run promotion section by section and reconcile the counts, rather than promoting the whole school in one action and hoping.
  • Handle the exceptions explicitly: students repeating a year, withdrawals, and transfer certificates issued over the break.
  • Confirm that last year's records are intact and still filterable — promotion creates a new enrolment, it does not overwrite history.

One week out: fees, staff and access

The three areas where a mistake in week one costs the whole term:

  • Fee structures for every class, including revised amounts, new heads, transport slabs, and any concessions or scholarships carried forward. Check the financial-year receipt numbering rolls over correctly.
  • Staff records — new appointments added, departures removed, subject and class assignments updated, leave balances opened for the year.
  • Roles and access — review every custom role against what people actually do this year, and revoke access for anyone who has left. This is the single most-skipped item on any back-to-school list.
  • Timetable — generate, then override the slots that carry a human reason, and publish before the first day rather than during it.

Automation: turn it on deliberately, one at a time

Scheduled automation in Schoolment — fee generation, promotion, exam creation — is off until an admin turns it on, and the start of the year is exactly when last year's settings deserve a second look rather than an assumption.

Enable one at a time and watch the first run. Fee generation first, because it is the most visible to parents and the easiest to verify against a handful of students. Everything that runs is logged, so an admin can confirm what happened and when instead of discovering it from a parent's phone call.

Day one: the parent side

For new families, the portal is their first real interaction with how the school operates, and first impressions here are durable. Three things worth doing in the opening week:

  • Link portal access for every new guardian and check the coverage figure rather than assuming — unlinked guardians are invisible to every channel you have.
  • Confirm phone numbers and email addresses are current, so fee reminders reach both WhatsApp and email.
  • Send one clear announcement explaining what parents can now do themselves — check fees, attendance, marks, timetable and homework, pay online, and raise leave requests — and point them at the Android and iOS app.

End of week two: the review nobody schedules

Set a calendar reminder now, because by week three the term has taken over. Check five things: fee generation produced the right amounts for a sample of students across classes; attendance is being marked in every section rather than a few; the timetable has no unreported clashes; the first automated reminders actually went out; and no staff member is working around the system with a private spreadsheet.

That last one is the most useful signal you will get all year. A workaround always points at either a permission that is too tight or a step that is genuinely harder than it should be, and both are fixable in week two far more cheaply than in month six. See Help Centre.

Rehearse a complete first day before opening the gates

Treat readiness as a sequence of tasks that a member of staff can complete, rather than a set of screens that an administrator has configured. Use a test student to check class allocation, attendance, the timetable, the first fee demand and the guardian view. A correct class list is not enough if the parent account still points to last year's section or a teacher cannot see the register they need.

Include a returning student, a new admission and a student changing transport routes in the rehearsal. These three cases expose different dependencies. Keep test records clearly labelled and remove or archive them through the normal administrative process before issuing real demands or messages. Ask each department to record its own result so the principal can see which problems still need attention.

Prepare an opening-week exception desk

The first week produces changes that could not have been settled in advance: a late admission, a family reporting a new phone number, a revised pickup arrangement or a teacher moving sections. Give these requests one destination and a named owner. A shared request log should record the student identifier, requested change, requester, approval where needed and completion status.

Avoid asking every teacher to solve the same access problem independently. Appoint a person who can distinguish an incorrect password from an unlinked guardian account or a missing permission. Publish office hours for routine setup support and a separate route for urgent attendance or transport concerns. This keeps classroom time from becoming an informal help desk.

Use a readiness checklist with evidence

  • Academic coordinator: open each section's timetable and check the assigned teacher, room and subject against the approved version.
  • Accounts team: compare a small sample of opening balances with the signed closing ledger, including a concession and a partial payment.
  • Class teachers: open their own attendance screens using their own accounts and confirm that transferred students are handled correctly.
  • Parent support: verify that a guardian can see only their linked children and can find the current term's notices.
  • Office administrator: check the route for correcting a record and confirm who covers it when the usual owner is absent.

Decide what can wait until week three

An unfinished optional module should not delay a sound launch of the essential daily workflows. Make a separate improvement list for additional reports, cosmetic report-card changes and older document uploads. Give each item a review date so postponement does not become abandonment. Keep the first week's priority on trustworthy student records, working access and reliable communication.

At the end of the second week, compare unresolved requests with the first-day log. Repeated password problems point to onboarding; repeated section corrections point to master data; repeated balance disputes point to migration. Fix the cause before adding more automation. The useful outcome of the review is a short list of changes with owners, not a general statement that the software is now live.

Opening-week readiness checks

Use these checks when reviewing the process with the people who will operate it. Record what was demonstrated and assign any unresolved issue to an owner before expanding the rollout.
