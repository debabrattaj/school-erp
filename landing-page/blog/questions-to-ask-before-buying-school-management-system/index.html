10 Questions to Ask Before Buying a School Management System

Ten direct questions to ask any school ERP vendor during a demo, before you commit — covering data, cost, support, and what happens if you leave.

Introduction

A software demonstration is the time to clarify not only what the system does, but what your school will need to configure, maintain and pay for. The questions below help structure that conversation. Use them to compare written answers from suppliers and identify anything that still needs a practical demonstration.

Ask these before you sign anything

  • Is our school's data in its own isolated database, or shared with other schools on the same infrastructure?
  • What exactly is included in the price — is migration from our current system extra? Is support extra after the first few months?
  • What happens to automated processes (fee generation, promotions, exam schedules) by default — do they run automatically, or does our admin have to switch each one on?
  • Can parents self-serve (check fees, attendance, marks) without calling our office?
  • How is our data secured — are passwords hashed, is traffic encrypted, who else at your company can see our records?
  • Can we export all of our own data if we ever want to leave?
  • How many people from your side will actually help with onboarding, and for how long?
  • What does a delayed or failed fee payment look like from the parent's side and from ours?
  • Is there a real human we can reach when something breaks, not just a ticket queue?
  • Can we try it with our own school's real data before committing, not just a demo account with sample students?

Why this list matters more than a feature comparison

Feature lists tend to look similar across vendors on paper. The questions above surface the things that actually differ — and the things that cause regret six months in, after the contract's signed and the migration's already happened.

Five more questions, asked after the first ten land well

The first ten cover the contract. These five cover the next three years:

  • What happens at the academic year boundary? Does promotion preserve last year's records as they were, or archive them somewhere you need help to reach?
  • Can we build our own reports? Or does every new question become a change request to your team?
  • Which modules are add-ons? Ask for the list in writing, so the quoted price is the price.
  • Are there per-transaction fees on fee collection, and where does the money settle? Direct settlement to the school's own account is not universal.
  • Can we customise the forms and roles ourselves? Renaming a field or building a new role should not require a release from the vendor.

How to tell a real answer from a rehearsed one

Vendors are practised at these questions. Three techniques that get past the script: ask for a demonstration rather than a statement ("show me the screen where an admin turns fee automation on"); ask what happens when it goes wrong ("show me a payment recorded against the wrong sibling being corrected"); and ask who else has this problem ("which of your schools has a fee structure like ours, and can we speak to them").

The most useful signal is not the answer — it is whether the person can answer without checking. Someone who has watched schools use the product answers immediately.

Ask what the product deliberately does not do

Any vendor who says their product does everything is either selling or has not been asked carefully. A confident answer to this question is a strong positive signal, because it means someone made scoping decisions on purpose.

For the record, ours: Schoolment carries the teaching side as well as the administrative one — courses with sequenced lessons and progress, study material, homework handed in and graded, and opt-in SCORM and discussion forums — but it does not host video files, so a video lesson is a link. Online tests, exam proctoring and biometric attendance are licensed add-ons rather than part of the base suite. We do not automate decisions about individual students. Get the answers in writing before the demo ends

The pattern to avoid is a good conversation, a signed contract, and a different set of facts in month three. Everything on both lists should end up in an email you keep — not because anyone is expected to act in bad faith, but because the salesperson you spoke to may not be there next year and a written answer survives them.

Four items in particular: what is included versus billed separately, the support commitment and its duration, the export process if you leave, and the data protection specifics — isolation, password hashing, transport encryption, retention. See Privacy & Security for how we answer the last one, and the comparison framework for scoring several vendors against the same sheet.

Ask what your staff can change without a support ticket

Schools regularly revise sections, fee assignments, report layouts and staff responsibilities. Ask which changes an authorised administrator can make and which require supplier assistance. Have the vendor show one ordinary change and one correction after records already exist. The distinction matters because a configuration that is easy before launch may be harder once bills or results have been published.

Record the limits as well as the capabilities. If a particular change needs supplier involvement, ask how the request is tracked and what information the school must provide. The goal is to understand daily independence and avoid discovering during a busy period that a routine adjustment depends on an unavailable external operator.

Ask how the system handles a wrong record

Use an example from your own office, expressed with fictional data. A student might be assigned the wrong fee category or an assessment might be entered against the wrong subject. Follow the correction through the ledger, report and guardian view. Check whether the original entry remains explainable and whether the person making the correction needs appropriate permission.

This question reveals more than a request to show an audit log. It tests whether the whole workflow supports accountable correction. Ask who approves the change under the proposed setup and what the affected family sees afterwards. A system should make the right correction possible without requiring staff to hide the error in a separate spreadsheet.

Ask for evidence you can review after the meeting

  • A written scope identifying included modules, user groups and optional charges.
  • A sample export with the fields and identifiers the school would receive.
  • A migration checklist stating who prepares, imports and validates each source.
  • A record of the demonstrated workflows and any unresolved acceptance conditions.
  • A support process that explains ownership, escalation and closure of a request.

Ask what happens if the pilot does not meet the requirements

Agree the purpose and boundaries of a trial before staff spend time preparing it. Identify the tasks being tested, the people reviewing the results and the issues that would prevent wider rollout. If a gap appears, decide whether it can be resolved through configuration, training or a product change. Do not quietly redefine success to match whatever the trial happens to deliver.

At the decision meeting, return to the original questions and mark which answers are supported by evidence. A confident sales answer is useful only when the school can connect it to a working feature or a clear written commitment. The final purchase decision should explain what was verified, which limits remain and why the arrangement fits the institution's daily work.

Questions that need evidence

Use these checks when reviewing the process with the people who will operate it. Record what was demonstrated and assign any unresolved issue to an owner before expanding the rollout.

Using this guide

The checklists and worked examples in this guide are recommended school procedures and evaluation questions. They are not a promise that every step is automatic. Confirm the modules enabled for your school and rehearse the workflow before relying on it.

Reviews & Approvals adds controlled result releases, payment exception review, register submission and saved evidence or timetable versions. Hosting, offsite backups, restoration tests, support response times and complete migration exports require separate deployment or service confirmation.
