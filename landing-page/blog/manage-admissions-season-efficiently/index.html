How to Manage Admissions Season Efficiently with an ERP

Admissions season creates a spike in enquiries and follow-ups. Here's how to handle that volume without enquiries slipping through the cracks.

Using this guide

The checklists and worked examples in this guide are recommended school procedures and evaluation questions. They are not a promise that every step is automatic. Confirm the modules enabled for your school and rehearse the workflow before relying on it.

After enquiry conversion, Reviews & Approvals can assign a class, create the enrolment, link an existing parent account and add selected fee structures. Document review records Received, Reviewed or Needs clarification. Creating and verifying the parent account remains a separate staff step.

The core problem is volume, not complexity

Admissions season isn't harder work per enquiry — it's the same simple process (respond, follow up, convert) repeated at a much higher volume, all at once. That's exactly the condition under which things get missed: an enquiry that arrives on a busy day and never gets a follow-up.

What reduces the chance of a missed enquiry

  • One pipeline for every enquiry, however it arrives — phone, walk-in, or an online form — instead of separate lists per channel.
  • A public apply-online form parents can fill out themselves, reducing the amount of manual data entry your office has to do during the busiest weeks.
  • Automatic confirmation the moment someone applies, so no one is left wondering if their enquiry went through.
  • Clear pipeline stages (Inquiry, Contacted, Enrolled) so it's obvious at a glance which enquiries still need a follow-up.

Map the funnel before you try to speed it up

Admissions is a funnel with five stages, and schools that struggle usually cannot say how many enquiries are sitting in each one on a given morning. The stages are: enquiry received, first contact made, application submitted, admission test or interaction completed, and enrolled. Every stage has a drop-off, and each drop-off has a different cause.

Once the pipeline is visible, the fix is usually obvious and small. A pile at "contacted but no application" means the form is too hard or nobody followed up. A pile at "applied but not tested" means scheduling is the bottleneck, not interest.

Duplicates and follow-up gaps are what actually lose admissions

Two failure modes account for most lost enquiries, and neither is about effort:

  • The same family entered three times. A phone enquiry on Monday, a website form on Wednesday, a walk-in on Saturday — three records, three half-conversations, and a parent who has explained their situation three times. Schoolment's duplicate detection catches these at entry.
  • The follow-up nobody owned. An enquiry with no next action and no owner will not be chased on the busiest week of the year. Pipeline tasks and automatic admission reminders make the next step visible rather than remembered.

Let parents do the data entry, then check it

The public online application form is the single highest-leverage piece in admission season, and not because it looks modern. It moves the typing from your office to the family, at the moment they are motivated, at whatever hour suits them — and it captures the details in the same structure your records use.

That matters beyond the season itself. Names, dates of birth and guardian numbers typed by a rushed clerk in April are the same names that appear misspelled on report cards in December and on transfer certificates years later. Admission tests can be scheduled and recorded against the same application, so a candidate's whole file — enquiry, form, documents, test result — sits in one place before any decision is made.

One click from applicant to student

The handover from admissions to academics is where paper systems lose data. Converting an enrolled applicant in Schoolment creates the student profile with an automatic admission number, carries the details already captured, and places the student in a class and section for the current academic year.

From that moment the same record drives everything downstream: fee structures for the new class, attendance from day one, portal access linking for the guardians, and document generation — bonafide certificates, ID cards, transfer certificates and transcripts — without re-typing anything. Schools admitting international students can hold the additional documents on the same profile.

What to review after the season closes

The end of admissions is the only time anyone will look at the data, so it is worth twenty minutes with the CRM analytics before it is forgotten:

  • Which sources produced enquiries, and which produced enrolments — these are rarely the same list.
  • Average time from enquiry to first contact, which is the number most correlated with conversion.
  • Where in the pipeline enquiries stalled, and whether it was the same stage as last year.
  • How many applications arrived through the online form versus the office, so next year's staffing reflects reality.
  • How many duplicates were caught, which tells you how much of the funnel was noise.

Define each stage by an observable event

Use stages that staff can apply consistently: enquiry received, visit scheduled, application submitted, documents checked, decision communicated and enrolment confirmed. Agree what moves a record from one stage to the next. A verbal expression of interest is different from a completed application, and an offer is different from a confirmed enrolment.

Keep a next action and an owner on every active record. An enquiry with no next action is effectively waiting for someone to remember it. Review overdue follow-ups at a regular interval and distinguish unreachable families from families awaiting a school decision. This helps the team focus attention without repeatedly contacting people who have already responded.

Work through a sibling and duplicate-enquiry example

A family may enquire by phone, submit a website form and visit the campus. If each interaction becomes a separate applicant, staff can send conflicting follow-ups and overstate demand. Match possible duplicates using appropriate identifiers and have an authorised person review uncertain cases. Do not merge solely because two children share a surname or guardian number.

For siblings, keep each child's application separate while linking the correct family contact. A decision about one child's class or documents should not overwrite the other's record. Test this scenario before the busy period, especially if staff import enquiries from several channels. The same exercise checks whether the system preserves the source and history of each interaction.

Prepare a document-review checklist

  • State which items are needed at each stage under the school's current admissions process.
  • Mark each item as received, reviewed or awaiting clarification rather than treating upload as approval.
  • Record who checked a document and how a correction request was communicated.
  • Avoid repeatedly requesting information the school already holds in the approved application record.
  • Restrict applicant documents to staff responsible for the admission and related authorised work.

Close the season with usable records

Once a place is confirmed, verify the transition into the student register, section, fee assignment and guardian access. Keep the accepted application and approval history available to the authorised office team. An efficient admissions season should not leave the academic team retyping everything after families have already completed the forms.

Review the funnel using consistent definitions: enquiries, submitted applications, offers and confirmed enrolments. Record why cases remain open instead of assuming every unfinished application represents a lost admission. Staff can then distinguish delays in document checks, unanswered school decisions and families who chose another option. Use those findings to improve next season's process and communication.

Admissions process checks

Use these checks when reviewing the process with the people who will operate it. Record what was demonstrated and assign any unresolved issue to an owner before expanding the rollout.
